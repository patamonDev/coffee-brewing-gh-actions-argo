A simple tour of coffee and the ways we brew it.

About Coffee

Coffee is made from the roasted seeds of the Coffea plant. The two main species are Arabica, prized for sweetness and complexity, and Robusta, which is bolder, more bitter and higher in caffeine. Flavor is shaped by origin, processing, roast level, grind size, and, of course, how you brew it.

Brewing Methods

Pour Over

Clean · Bright · Nuanced

Hot water is poured by hand over grounds in a paper filter (V60, Chemex, Kalita). Great for highlighting delicate flavors.

  • Grind: medium-fine
  • Ratio: 1:16 (coffee:water)
  • Time: 2.5–4 min

French Press

Full-bodied · Rich · Rustic

Grounds steep in hot water, then a metal mesh plunger presses them down. Oils stay in the cup for a heavy body.

  • Grind: coarse
  • Ratio: 1:15
  • Time: 4 min steep

Espresso

Intense · Syrupy · Crema

Near-boiling water is forced through finely ground coffee at about 9 bars of pressure. The base for lattes, cappuccinos and more.

  • Grind: fine
  • Ratio: 1:2
  • Time: 25–30 sec

AeroPress

Versatile · Smooth · Portable

A syringe-like brewer that combines immersion and pressure. Fast, forgiving, and easy to experiment with.

  • Grind: medium-fine
  • Ratio: 1:12 to 1:16
  • Time: 1–2 min

Moka Pot

Strong · Bold · Stovetop

Steam pressure pushes water up through the grounds on the stove, producing a concentrated, espresso-like coffee.

  • Grind: medium-fine
  • Ratio: about 1:10
  • Time: 4–5 min

Cold Brew

Smooth · Sweet · Low acidity

Coarse grounds steep in cold water for many hours, then get filtered. Serve over ice or dilute the concentrate.

  • Grind: coarse
  • Ratio: 1:8 (concentrate)
  • Time: 12–18 hours

Quick Tips

  • Use fresh beans and grind right before brewing.
  • Weigh your coffee and water with a scale for consistency.
  • Water at roughly 90–96°C (195–205°F) works for most methods.
  • Too bitter? Grind coarser. Too sour? Grind finer.
